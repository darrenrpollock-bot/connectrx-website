Onboarding & Adoption

The six-week onboarding programme

GekoPulse doesn't ship as a login and a training video. Every rollout runs through a deliberate six-week programme where ConnectRx treats your sales team as the customer we have to win — because a competitive field intelligence platform is only worth what the field actually puts into it.

Why adoption is the hard part

The software is the easy bit. What defeats most field intelligence and CRM rollouts is the same thing every time: the tool goes live, a training session happens, and adoption is quietly handed to the client to sort out. Usage spikes for a fortnight and then decays. Within a quarter the data is thin, the picture is stale, and leadership is back to making decisions on instinct.

Crayon's 2026 State of Competitive Intelligence report puts a number on the cost of that: teams that share competitive intelligence weekly or faster achieve positive revenue impact at 79%, against 41% for monthly-or-slower. The gap between winning and not isn't the quality of any single insight — it's whether capture and distribution are a routine. Routines have to be built. They don't arrive with the software.

"A field intelligence platform is only ever worth what the field actually puts into it. Getting that habit established is the job — so we do it, not you."

Your sales team is our customer

The practical consequence of that principle: for six weeks, your reps and managers are the people we're trying to win over, and adoption is the outcome we hold ourselves accountable for — measured and reported, not assumed.

That means we're not just standing up an account and walking away. We work alongside your managers so the manager command centre becomes part of the weekly rhythm — reading themes, actioning alerts, briefing the field. We work alongside your reps so a voice memo after a visit stops being a task and becomes reflex. And we calibrate, together, what strong field intelligence actually looks like for your market, so the signal that comes back is worth acting on.

What the six weeks are for

The programme moves through three intents, each building on the last:

  • Get it live and genuinely in use — every rep contributing real intelligence, every manager comfortable in the command centre, with the friction found and removed early.
  • Turn use into habit — the capture routine embedded into how the week already runs, reinforced by the feedback loop of intelligence coming back as sharper questions for the next visit.
  • Hand management a running system — a team that keeps contributing after we step back, and a leadership view of the field that's live rather than quarterly.

Throughout, adoption is tracked week by week and reviewed with management, so there's never a question of whether it's landing — you can see it.

What management walks away with

  • A team that's genuinely using it — adoption tracked week by week, not assumed
  • Managers fluent in the command centre: reading themes, actioning alerts, briefing the field
  • The capture habit embedded — a voice memo after a visit becomes automatic
  • A shared baseline for what strong field intelligence looks like in your market
  • By week six, a clear view of what the field is telling you that you couldn't see before

How it fits the partner programme

The six-week onboarding is part of what a foundation partner gets, not a paid add-on. We're onboarding a first cohort of medical device and pharma teams on introductory partner pricing, and the programme is how we make sure that early group succeeds — because their results are how GekoPulse earns the next cohort.
